# Diagnosing misconceptions from wrong answers and building a concept map with Gemini: research summary and pipeline for 문제노트

## 1. What the research shows

**Eedi "Mining Misconceptions in Mathematics" (Kaggle, Sept–Dec 2024)**
- **Task.** The input is a math MCQ plus one wrong option. The output is a ranked list of up to 25 misconceptions, scored with MAP@25.
- **Data.** About 1,857 questions, a closed list of 2,587 expert-written misconceptions, and many test-set misconceptions that never appeared in training.
- **What the winners did.** Every top team used retrieve-then-rerank.
  - 1st place (Raja Biswas, team MTH 101) used three fine-tuned retrievers (e5-mistral-7B, bge-en-icl, Qwen2.5-14B). They were chosen by recall@32, not MAP; the author found those two metrics often moved in opposite directions.
  - It then used pointwise rerankers (Qwen 14B and 32B) and a 72B listwise reranker. The rerankers were fed short "why a student would pick this" rationales generated by Claude.
  - Training data was about 10.6k synthetic MCQs (made with Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o, judged by GPT-4o) plus 1.8k real ones.
  - Other teams added LLM-written explanations of each misconception to the text being embedded. Even a 22M-parameter MiniLM with no fine-tuning improved a lot that way. 3rd place down-weighted misconceptions already seen in training.
- **Scores.**
  - The public baseline notebook scored about 0.45 private MAP@25.
  - A 164th-place two-stage Qwen-32B system scored 0.456.
  - A silver-medal (top 5%) system scored about 0.50. Its retriever alone had MAP@25 0.42 and recall@25 0.81.
  - I could not confirm the winning private score from the sources I could read.
- **Open-ended inference is harder still.** In the MISTAKE paper, inferring the misconception from a wrong answer gave MAP@25 of only 0.178 → 0.204 with an 8B model. Even GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 struggled to simulate students who hold a given misconception.
- **Lessons that transfer to a small setup.**
  1. Explaining why a distractor is attractive (a rationale) is the strongest signal.
  2. Choosing from a short candidate list beats open retrieval over thousands of labels.
  3. Measure recall at the candidate stage before precision.
  4. Generic LLMs are weak on distractors out of the box. Ground them in author explanations.

**The size of the candidate list decides accuracy.** Liu et al. asked GPT-4 to name the misconception behind a wrong answer. With 4 candidates (1 true, 3 random) it reached 91.9% accuracy. With 100 candidates it fell to 39.8%. Otero et al. reached 83.9% precision only when the task was restricted to one topic and used educator feedback. **Design rule: always give Gemini a small, chapter-scoped candidate set (about 8–15 concepts), never the whole course.**

**LLMs write weaker distractor rationales than people.**
- Feng et al. (NAACL 2024) found LLMs produce mathematically valid distractors but are poor at predicting the errors real students make.
- DiVERT (EMNLP 2024) found GPT-4o's error explanations were significantly worse than human-written ones and worse than a fine-tuned 7B model.
- For us this means: when the item's explanation already names a trap ("함정: …"), use it as ground truth. Treat a purely inferred label as a hypothesis that must pass verification.

**Hidden misconceptions behind correct answers.**
- Couch et al. (BioScience 2018): nearly half of students who pick the correct MC answer still hold wrong beliefs about the other options.
- Imran and Bulathwela (2026), on Eedi data, separated wrong answers from correct-but-flawed reasoning:
  - A fine-tuned BERT caught only 57% of correct-answer misconceptions.
  - Gemini 3 Flash caught 70.5%, with 8.9% false positives on genuinely correct students.
  - At realistic prevalence, false alarms outnumbered real detections by about 8 to 1.
  - They recommend graded categories (clear / needs clarification / misconception) and a detect → verify → follow-up-question flow instead of binary verdicts.
- **Implication:** a correct MC answer is weak evidence of mastery. A correct answer only partly rules out a misconception.

**Automatic concept (KC) tagging and Q-matrix construction.**
- Moore et al. (L@S 2024), GPT-4 on MCQs:
  - Matched the human concept tag on 56% of Chemistry items and 35% of E-Learning items, higher when the top 5 were allowed. The "simulated textbook" prompt worked best.
  - When the LLM and the human disagreed, experts preferred the LLM's tag 57 of 87 times (p = 0.017).
- Wang, Lin and Koedinger (2025), 646 MCQs:
  - Open generation produced 569 concepts against 101 in the expert model, and fit student learning data worse.
  - Merging labels with embedding cosine ≥ 0.8 helped. A threshold of 0.7 merged too aggressively.
  - **Lesson: build a fixed vocabulary first, then assign items to it.**
- KCluster (EDM 2025): LLM-similarity clustering of questions matched or beat expert concept models on learning-curve fit (item-blocked RMSE). Validate with student data once it exists.
- 2403.17281, LLM judging whether a concept matches a question:
  - GPT-4 had accuracy .91 but precision only .48 at a 1:8 match imbalance, so models over-tag.
  - Adding expert-written definitions and boundaries for each concept helped every model.
  - A self-check applied only to positive matches, worded neutrally, raised precision. Skeptical wording ("are you sure?") flipped correct answers.

**True/false (OX) items.** The statement itself carries the diagnosis.
- If the statement is false (planted error) and the learner answers O, they believe the planted error. The misconception is that error, and the fix is the true version.
- If the statement is true and the learner answers X, they reject a true fact. This only says "this concept is weak"; it does not say which wrong belief they hold.
- Guessing is 50%, so OX evidence carries half the weight of MC. Multiple-true-false research supports OX for spotting mixed understanding.
- Three-tier tests add a confidence tier because a wrong answer alone cannot separate "doesn't know" from "holds a wrong belief." In our app, the "모르겠어요" button and very fast answers play that role.

**Short answer.**
- Bug libraries (Brown and Burton's BUGGY; Brown and VanLehn's repair theory) list systematic wrong procedures and the wrong answers each one produces.
- LLMs classify error types reasonably on coarse classes (McNichols et al. 2023), but fine-grained taxonomies stay below 30% accuracy (MathCCS). They work much better when told the correct answer and that the response is wrong (2501.15581).
- Some different bugs produce the same wrong value; McNichols et al. found reversed-sides and wrong-operation errors were the most confused pair. Store that ambiguity instead of forcing a single label.

**Essays.** LLM analytic grading agrees with humans best on checklist-style criteria and worst on evaluative ones. Rubrics that mirror human grading logic shrink the gap. So the existing covered/partial/missing verdicts per point are good concept evidence, once each point is mapped to a concept. Weight them below the user's own verdict when the two disagree.

**Distractor statistics, once more learners join.**
- Classical distractor analysis: the correct option's choice should correlate positively with total score, and a working distractor negatively.
- A distractor near zero or positive means the option or the answer key is flawed; send it to admin.
- A distractor chosen by under about 5% of learners does no work.
- The NeurIPS 2020 Eedi challenge was built on the premise that the specific option chosen is predictable and reveals the misconception.

**Learner modelling with little data.**
- Elo-style updates (Pelánek 2016) are simple and robust for adaptive practice.
- Knowledge-tracing guess and slip parameters supply the right priors (guess capped around 0.3).
- Rapid-guess detection by response time: one common rule flags answers faster than 10% of the item's mean time.

## 2. Recommended pipeline

**Offline, run once per problem version.**
1. **(A) Concept vocabulary per course.**
   - Gemini proposes concepts per chapter; a course-level pass merges them into 15–40 Korean concepts. Each concept gets a definition, a boundary, prerequisites, and a list of concepts it is easily confused with.
   - The developer approves the list in the admin page.
   - Freeze it as `concept_vocab(course_id, vocab_ver)`.
2. **(B) Item tagging.**
   - For each problem version, send the chapter's concepts plus their confusable neighbours as a dynamic enum (8–15 IDs).
   - Output: primary/secondary concepts plus a diagnosis per wrong option, per OX direction, per anticipated short-answer error, or per essay point.
3. **(C) Verification, preferably with a different model** (gemini-2.5-flash tags, flash-lite verifies, or the reverse).
   - **Cycle check:** given only the misconception text, which option would that student pick? It must match the tagged option.
   - **Neutral concept-fit check:** only on assigned concepts.
   - Store `verified` and `conf`.
4. **Storage and re-tagging.**
   - Store in `item_tag(problem_id, version, body_hash, vocab_ver, prompt_ver, model, tag_json, conf, verified, created_at)`.
   - Re-tag only when `body_hash` or `vocab_ver` changes; an import triggers a diff.
5. **Calibrate on 공학수학 first.** Its existing `topic` and `tags` fields are free ground truth. Measure Gemini's primary-concept agreement there, then roll out to the other four courses.

**Online, per learner. Pure JS/SQL, no LLM.**
- **Evidence unit:** one (item, attempt) pair.
  - Weights:
    - Attempt: first 1.0, later 0.5.
    - Response time: rapid guess 0.3, using ms < max(3 s, 10% of item median, or of the kind's median until per-item data exists).
    - "모르겠어요" (idk): counts as a concept gap with weight 1 but never as a misconception.
    - Self-graded essays: 0.7.
  - Distinct-item rule: each item contributes at most once to a given misconception.
- **Concept mastery:** a Beta posterior per concept. A correct answer adds (1 − guess) × w to α, where guess is MC 0.25, OX 0.5, short 0.05. A wrong answer adds w to β. Show "근거 부족" when there are fewer than 3 items.
- **Misconception score:**
  - S_m = Σ over distinct items hit of w·(d/3), minus 0.5·Σ over items where the same trap was offered and avoided of w·(d/3). Here d is diagnosticity from 0 to 3.
  - Report only when the learner hit it on 2 or more distinct items and S_m ≥ 1.2. Prefer hits spread across sessions or chapters.
- **Confusion pairs ("A를 B와 헷갈림"):** aggregate unordered (concept, confused_with) pairs over distinct items with the same thresholds. Hits in both directions (A for B and B for A) are stronger evidence.
- **Population blending, once an option has 20–30 or more responders:**
  - Compute point-biserial distractor discrimination and selection rate.
  - Blend: d_eff = (k·d_llm + n·d_emp)/(k + n), with k = 20.
  - Flag items whose correct option discriminates negatively (suspect answer key).
- **Imported progress with no events:** use `last_answer` when it is an MC letter or O/X. Otherwise the row counts only for concept mastery, at weight 0.5.
- **Report text:**
  - One Gemini call per course, on demand, cached by (device, course, last event id, vocab_ver).
  - Input is compact evidence JSON: top concepts with posteriors, misconceptions or pairs with the item excerpts and options actually chosen, and avoided traps.
  - The model must only explain and prioritise what is in that evidence and cite item IDs. Templates are impossible because the content is the evidence itself.
  - For "needs clarification" cases, have it propose one follow-up check question per weak concept (variant-problem strategy).

## 3. Prompt skeletons (Gemini `responseSchema`, temperature 0)

Use short concept IDs (`c01`…) in enums; the documentation warns that large or long enums can return 400. The enum is built per request from the chapter's candidates. The docs I fetched did not mention `propertyOrdering`; other Google pages disagree on whether key order is followed, so set it explicitly so the model writes the reasoning field before the label (generation follows field order).

### (a) Concept vocabulary

Run once per chapter, then one course-level merge pass.

```
SYSTEM: 너는 대학 강의 '{course}'의 개념 지도를 설계하는 교수자다.
- 입력: 챕터 제목, 개념요약(있으면), 기존 tags/topic 빈도, 문항 줄기·해설 발췌(특히 "함정:" 문장).
- 개념은 '시험에서 틀릴 수 있는 이해 단위'로 정의한다. 사례·수치·인명·연도는 개념이 아니다(사례는 도구일 뿐).
- 챕터당 2~6개, 이름은 한국어 20자 이내 명사구. 정의 1~2문장, 포함하지 않는 것(boundary) 1문장.
- 헷갈리기 쉬운 개념쌍은 해설의 함정 문장에 근거가 있을 때만 적는다.
- 같은 뜻 다른 표현은 하나로 합친다.
```
```json
{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
 "concepts":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
   "id":{"type":"STRING"},"name":{"type":"STRING"},"chapter":{"type":"INTEGER"},
   "definition":{"type":"STRING"},"boundary":{"type":"STRING"},
   "prereq":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"STRING"}},
   "confusable":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
      "id":{"type":"STRING"},"why":{"type":"STRING"}},"required":["id","why"]}},
   "support_items":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"STRING"}}},
  "required":["id","name","chapter","definition","boundary"]}}},
 "required":["concepts"]}
```

**Merge pass.** Input: all chapter proposals. Task: dedupe, keep 15–40 concepts, renumber, and preserve support_items. Output the same schema plus `merged_from`. Drop any concept with fewer than 2 support items, or fold it into a neighbour.

### (b) Item and distractor tagging

```
SYSTEM: 너는 '{course}' 문항의 진단 설계자다. 아래 개념 목록에서만 고른다(목록 밖 개념 금지).
[개념 목록: id | 이름 | 정의 | 경계 | 헷갈리는 개념]
규칙:
1. 먼저 정답 근거를 해설(s)에서 찾아 rationale에 적는다.
2. 각 오답 선지마다 "이 선지를 고르는 학생이 믿고 있는 것"을 학습자 관점 한 문장으로("~라고 생각함") 쓴다.
   해설에 함정 설명이 있으면 그것을 따르고 source="explanation", 없으면 source="inferred".
3. diagnosticity: 3=특정 오개념이 있어야만 고름, 2=오개념 가능성 높음, 1=부분지식/조건 오독, 0=무작위·실수와 구분 불가.
4. confused_with는 해당 선지가 '다른 개념의 성질을 이 개념에 적용'할 때만.
5. 개념은 primary 1개, secondary 최대 2개. 확실하지 않으면 secondary를 비운다.
OX: 진술의 참/거짓, 거짓이면 틀린 부분(planted_error)과 올바른 진술, 그리고 반대로 답했을 때의 함의.
단답: 예상 오답 패턴(부호·공식혼동·단위·정의·전치·계산) 최대 4개, 각 패턴이 낳는 오답 예시.
서술: 각 채점 포인트(pts)를 개념 id에 매핑.
```
```json
{"type":"OBJECT","propertyOrdering":["rationale","concepts","options","ox","short_errors","essay_points"],
 "properties":{
  "rationale":{"type":"STRING"},
  "concepts":{"type":"ARRAY","maxItems":3,"items":{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
     "id":{"type":"STRING","enum":["<dynamic ids>"]},"role":{"type":"STRING","enum":["primary","secondary"]}},
     "required":["id","role"]}},
  "options":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
     "idx":{"type":"INTEGER"},"misconception":{"type":"STRING"},
     "concept":{"type":"STRING","enum":["<dynamic ids>"]},
     "confused_with":{"type":"STRING","enum":["none","<dynamic ids>"]},
     "error_type":{"type":"STRING","enum":["concept_confusion","overgeneralize","condition_misread","partial_knowledge","calc_slip","weak_distractor"]},
     "diagnosticity":{"type":"INTEGER","enum":[0,1,2,3]},
     "source":{"type":"STRING","enum":["explanation","inferred"]}},
     "required":["idx","misconception","concept","confused_with","error_type","diagnosticity","source"]}},
  "ox":{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
     "statement_true":{"type":"BOOLEAN"},"planted_error":{"type":"STRING"},"correct_version":{"type":"STRING"},
     "wrong_implies":{"type":"STRING"},"concept":{"type":"STRING","enum":["<dynamic ids>"]},
     "confused_with":{"type":"STRING","enum":["none","<dynamic ids>"]},
     "diagnosticity":{"type":"INTEGER","enum":[0,1,2,3]}}},
  "short_errors":{"type":"ARRAY","maxItems":4,"items":{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
     "pattern":{"type":"STRING"},"example_wrong":{"type":"STRING"},
     "error_type":{"type":"STRING","enum":["sign","formula_confusion","unit","definition","transposition","calc"]},
     "concept":{"type":"STRING","enum":["<dynamic ids>"]}}}},
  "essay_points":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
     "point_index":{"type":"INTEGER"},"concept":{"type":"STRING","enum":["<dynamic ids>"]}}}}},
 "required":["rationale","concepts"]}
```

Only send the block for the item's `kind` (drop the other properties) to keep the schema small. OX items are short, so 3–5 can be batched per call with an `items[]` wrapper. For short answers, match a learner's wrong text at runtime against `example_wrong` (numeric tolerance from `tol`). If nothing matches, fall back to concept-level evidence. An LLM classifier for typed answers can be added later as an on-demand call, and it should be told the correct answer.

### (c) Verification

Use a second model, temperature 0, with neutral wording.

```
SYSTEM: 아래 문항과 '학생 설명'이 주어진다. 이 학생이 실제로 고를 가능성이 가장 높은 선지 하나를 고르고 이유를 한 문장으로 써라.
(정답 표시 없음. 학생 설명 = options[j].misconception)
이어서 각 개념에 대해: "이 문항을 풀 때 이 개념의 이해가 필요한가?" yes/no와 근거 한 문장.
```
```json
{"type":"OBJECT","properties":{
 "cycle":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"OBJECT","propertyOrdering":["reason","picked_idx"],"properties":{
    "misc_ref":{"type":"INTEGER"},"reason":{"type":"STRING"},"picked_idx":{"type":"INTEGER"}},
    "required":["misc_ref","reason","picked_idx"]}},
 "concept_fit":{"type":"ARRAY","items":{"type":"OBJECT","propertyOrdering":["reason","fit"],"properties":{
    "id":{"type":"STRING"},"reason":{"type":"STRING"},"fit":{"type":"STRING","enum":["yes","no"]}},
    "required":["id","reason","fit"]}}},
 "required":["cycle","concept_fit"]}
```

**How the results set confidence.**
- Option label: the cycle check passes, or source is "explanation" → `conf` = high.
- If the cycle check fails, re-tag that option once. If it fails again, set diagnosticity = min(d, 1) and the option counts only as concept evidence.
- Concept fit "no" → drop a secondary concept. If the primary concept gets "no", send the item to the admin review queue.
- OX: re-derive `statement_true` from the stored ox key. Any mismatch means the tag is broken, so re-tag.

## 4. Expected accuracy and failure modes

**Accuracy estimates.** These are extrapolations from the studies above, not measurements. Measure on 공학수학 first.
- **Primary concept, closed vocabulary of 8–15 candidates with definitions:** likely 80–90% agreement with the 공학수학 gold. Secondary-concept precision will be lower, because LLMs over-tag.
- **Option misconception labels:**
  - Grounded in "함정:" text: high.
  - Inferred only: roughly 60–75% would be judged acceptable. The cycle check filters the worst, but even frontier models simulate wrong students poorly.
- **Learner-level diagnosis:** precision comes mainly from the "2 or more distinct items" and "avoided-trap" rules, not from per-item accuracy.

**Failure modes and mitigations.**
1. **Over-granular or duplicate concepts.** Fixed vocabulary, merge pass, 15–40 cap.
2. **Hallucinated rationales on weak distractors.** Diagnosticity 0–1 options never produce a misconception sentence in the report.
3. **OX "true statement marked X".** Only report a weak concept; never invent a wrong belief.
4. **Correct-answer trap.** Do not show mastery from MC alone. Suggest OX or variant follow-ups.
5. **Rapid guesses and slips.** Down-weight by response time.
6. **Imported history lacks options and ms.** Use `last_answer` when possible; otherwise concept-only evidence.
7. **One heavy user, so no population statistics yet.** Keep LLM priors until n ≥ 20–30 per option; key-error flags start then.
8. **LaTeX and math.** Send the MathJax source, and tell the model to treat it as math.
9. **Gemini 503s.** Use the existing fallback chain. Tagging is a resumable job (`tag_job` table, one item per row, skip done hashes).
10. **Report LLM inventing advice.** The report schema requires `evidence_item_ids` per claim; drop any claim whose IDs are not in the input.

**Cost and optimization.**
- About 1,198 problems × (1 tag + 1 verify), minus OX batching, is roughly 1,800–2,400 calls once.
- Throttle to stay under the free-tier per-project RPM/RPD (check the live limits in AI Studio; RPD resets at midnight Pacific).
- Run per course over several days.
- Learner reports are O(events): computed in SQL+JS and cached by last event id. The LLM is used only for the optional narrative, one call per course on demand.
